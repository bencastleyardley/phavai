Garmin Forerunner 265 vs COROS PACE 3

Choose the Garmin Forerunner 265 if you want the richer training ecosystem, AMOLED display, streaming-service music downloads, and more polished race-planning tools. Choose the COROS PACE 3 if low weight, long GPS battery, and value matter more than a bright AMOLED screen or Garmin's deeper feature stack. Both are capable marathon watches; this is mainly a software-and-priority decision.

The short answer: Garmin for the ecosystem, COROS for efficiency

The Forerunner 265 is the easier default for runners who want training readiness, training status, PacePro, race-day planning, an AMOLED touchscreen, and phone-free music from supported services. The PACE 3 covers the core running job with dual-frequency GPS, breadcrumb and turn-by-turn navigation, training plans, recovery tools, local audio support, and much longer standard-GPS battery in a lighter package.

Battery is the PACE 3's clearest advantage

Garmin rates the 46 mm Forerunner 265 for up to 20 hours in GPS-only mode, 16 hours with SatIQ AutoSelect, and 14 hours with all-systems multi-band tracking. COROS rates the PACE 3 for 38 hours in standard full GPS and 15 hours in dual-frequency mode. Those are laboratory estimates, not guarantees: music, navigation, backlight, sensors, satellite conditions, and battery age can shorten runtime.

Garmin wins when training guidance and music matter

Garmin's advantage is not basic run recording; both watches do that well. It is the density of connected training and race features, plus support for downloaded music from services such as Spotify, Deezer, and Amazon Music with the appropriate subscription. COROS provides workouts, training plans, recovery analysis, Training Hub, and built-in audio support, but its interface and ecosystem are intentionally leaner.

Neither watch is a full-map navigation watch

The Forerunner 265 supports course and breadcrumb-style navigation but does not include the full-color built-in maps found on the Forerunner 965. The PACE 3 provides breadcrumb routes, turn-by-turn prompts, elevation profiles, checkpoints, distance-to-destination, and deviation alerts. For unfamiliar mountain routes where map detail matters, move up to a watch with offline maps rather than choosing between these two.

Quick answers

Garmin Forerunner 265 vs COROS PACE 3 FAQ

Is the Garmin Forerunner 265 or COROS PACE 3 better for marathon training?

The Forerunner 265 is the stronger all-around default if you will use Garmin's training, recovery, race-planning, and music features. The PACE 3 is the better value if long battery life, low weight, and a simpler running workflow matter more.

Which watch has better GPS battery life?

COROS quotes up to 38 hours in standard full-GPS mode and 15 hours in dual-frequency mode. Garmin quotes up to 20 hours in GPS-only mode and 14 hours in all-systems multi-band mode for the 46 mm Forerunner 265. Actual results vary with settings and conditions.

Does either watch have full maps?

No. Both support planned-route navigation, but neither has the full offline mapping found on higher-end watches. The PACE 3 adds turn-by-turn and deviation tools; the Forerunner 265 uses course and breadcrumb-style navigation.

Can either watch play music without a phone?

Yes. Garmin supports on-watch storage and downloads from selected music services with the appropriate subscription. COROS supports local audio files. Music use reduces battery life on both.
